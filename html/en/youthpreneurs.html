Turning Entrepreneurial Ideas into Opportunities

From 14 to 24 November 2024, Asociația Inițiative Sociale (AIS) hosted the international youth exchange “NextGEN: YouthPreneurs” (A3) in Argestru, Vatra Dornei, Romania. It was the third mobility carried out under the Erasmus+ accreditation in the field of youth (2022-1-RO01-KA150-YOU-000110034), through project 2024-1-RO01-KA151-YOU-000218341.

The mobility brought together 55 people from 8 countries (Bulgaria, Czechia, Italy, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Spain and Turkey): 45 young people aged 18 to 30, 8 group leaders and 2 facilitators. Inclusion was an important part of the project: 14 young people with fewer opportunities took part (31.1% of all young participants) and gained access to international opportunities for learning, entrepreneurial development and intercultural cooperation.

About the mobility

The main aim of the mobility was to develop an entrepreneurial spirit and the skills needed to turn innovative ideas into viable economic initiatives, promoting entrepreneurship as a path to professional development and a way to fight youth unemployment.

The project set out to encourage creativity, initiative and entrepreneurial thinking, to develop business planning and management skills, to improve communication and cooperation, and to strengthen solidarity, tolerance and intercultural dialogue.

Over 9 days of educational activities, the participants took part in an intensive non-formal education programme based on experiential learning, workshops, brainstorming, business simulations, role play, debates, presentations, work in multicultural teams, reflection and participatory evaluation.

The activities included team-building and inclusion sessions, presentations on youth entrepreneurship in the eight countries (Youth Business in My Country), entrepreneurial challenges (The Entrepreneurial Challenge), idea-generation exercises and hands-on workshops on building practice firms (YouthPreneurs in Action).

A central part of the learning process was a business development simulation, in which participants went through the stages of turning an idea into a business concept: spotting opportunities, market analysis, choosing a target group, branding, financial planning, marketing and promotion.

The programme also included study visits and meetings with local entrepreneurs in the Vatra Dornei area, giving the young people a chance to discover real business models, the challenges of running a business and the importance of making good use of local resources.

Intercultural activities, presentations by the partner organisations and sessions on Erasmus+ and Youthpass completed the learning process and helped develop social and intercultural skills.

A3 produced concrete educational results and materials, developed with the direct involvement of the participants:

  • 8 case studies on youth entrepreneurship in the participating countries;
  • 7 practice firms, developed by multicultural teams;
  • 7 business plans, with market analyses, marketing strategies, financial plans and business pitch presentations;
  • 7 promotional videos made to promote the simulated firms;
  • 1 educational brochure dedicated to the mobility;
  • photos, videos, articles, interviews and participant testimonials, used to promote the results;
  • 8 national reports on dissemination and use of the results, prepared by the participating organisations.

Through these activities, the 45 young people gained practical knowledge of starting and running a business, spotting entrepreneurial opportunities, developing business models, planning resources and promoting products or services.

The participants also developed their skills in communication, leadership, negotiation, creativity, critical thinking, problem-solving, teamwork, adaptability and using digital tools.

The intercultural activities helped improve communication in English, boost self-confidence and develop the ability to work with people from different cultural backgrounds.

The learning process was monitored through daily reflections and evaluations, and the 45 young people had the skills they gained recognised with the Youthpass certificate.

The mobility was coordinated by AIS together with its 7 international partner organisations, through a structured system of planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

The preparation phase included selecting and preparing the participants, signing the partnership agreements, designing the educational programme, arranging travel and accommodation at Pensiunea Siady in Argestru, Vatra Dornei, and preparing information, learning and visibility materials.

Management was supported by digital communication and collaboration tools, coordination meetings and continuous monitoring of the activities. During the mobility, meetings with group leaders, daily reflections and the initial, mid-term and final evaluations made it possible to identify participants’ needs and adapt the learning process.

Special attention was paid to including young people with fewer opportunities, active participation, a safe environment and environmentally responsible practices.

The post-mobility evaluation, based on 53 completed questionnaires, showed a high level of satisfaction and a positive contribution to personal, social and entrepreneurial development.

Satisfaction and skills development (scale from 1 to 4 points, where 4 is the highest score):

  • Overall satisfaction: 3.75 out of 4 points.
  • Contribution to personal development: 3.58 out of 4 points.
  • Ability to work in multicultural teams: 3.64 out of 4 points.
  • Usefulness of the business plan workshops: 3.55 out of 4 points.
  • Contribution of the study visit to understanding entrepreneurship: 3.38 out of 4 points.
  • Confidence in creating and promoting a business: 3.38 out of 4 points.
  • Support from the host organisation: 3.77 out of 4 points.

Positive changes and skills gained (53 respondents):

  • 98.1% of respondents (52 of 53) noticed positive personal changes after taking part in the project.
  • 90.6% of respondents (48 of 53) clearly confirmed that the educational materials and worksheets helped develop their entrepreneurial skills.
  • 94.3% of respondents (50 of 53) felt the project encouraged a sense of community and collaboration among participants.
  • 96.2% of respondents (51 of 53) said they would like to take part in similar Erasmus+ projects in the future.

One of the most relevant results of the mobility was the participants’ increased motivation to turn business ideas into initiatives of their own.

In the post-mobility evaluation, 88.7% of respondents (47 of 53) explicitly said they want to start a business in the future.

This shows a growing interest in entrepreneurship and a readiness to put the skills gained to use in the future.

In concrete terms, by developing the 7 practice firms the participants went through the stages of starting a business, from spotting an opportunity to drawing up the financial plan and pitching the concept to an audience.

These activities helped build confidence in their own ideas, planning skills, a sense of responsibility and personal initiative.

As for young people who actually tried to start a business after the mobility, the available evaluations do not record a verifiable number of people who took concrete steps to launch one. The figure of 47 respondents reflects declared entrepreneurial intent, not the number of businesses started or registered.

Feedback from the 8 group leaders was positive: 6 leaders said they were “Very satisfied” and the other 2 “Satisfied” with the mobility.

All eight leaders confirmed that the participants developed entrepreneurial skills, and seven saw progress in personal and social skills and in the ability to learn.

Six leaders also noted better digital and multilingual skills, and six felt the participants had clarified their career aspirations and goals.

The partner organisations’ evaluation confirmed the quality of management and cooperation, with the following results:

  • 5 out of 5 points for communication with the partner organisations;
  • 4.86 out of 5 points for meeting the objectives;
  • 5 out of 5 points for the participants’ overall satisfaction, as rated by the partners.

The mobility also helped build AIS’s organisational capacity, strengthen international relationships and share methods and good practice between the participating organisations.

The results of A3 were promoted through the NextGEN blog, social media, the educational brochure, videos and multiplier activities organised by participants and partner organisations.

For this mobility, 8 national dissemination reports were produced, documenting presentations, workshops, meetings and online activities in the participating countries.

The documented activities include:

  • Romania: a presentation at the AHA Youth Centre in Bucharest, with 43 participants, and an event at the “George Emil Palade” High School in Constanța, with 30 participants.
  • Bulgaria: a presentation of the results at an educational institution, with 40 participants.
  • Portugal: three information and multiplier activities in Lisbon, Coimbra and Madeira, with 12 reported attendances.
  • Czechia: an educational presentation for 5 people, plus online promotion through the partner organisation.
  • Turkey: two dissemination events in Ankara, with 15 reported attendances.
  • Spain: three online sessions presenting the experiences and learning results, with 13 reported attendances.
  • Italy: an online dissemination session with 10 participants, plus digital promotion materials.

These activities add up to at least 168 reported attendances at the information and dissemination events identified for A3, plus online campaigns and materials shared through the networks of the organisations and participants.

Online dissemination included articles, testimonials, photos, videos and posts on Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and TikTok.

A good example of digital impact came from Italy, where a post made with the platforms Orientiamoci and Geopolitica.info reached 5,320 Instagram accounts, generating about 200 likes and 15 requests for information about Erasmus+.

The dissemination activities raised the project’s visibility, promoted Erasmus+ opportunities and spread non-formal education methods in the partner organisations’ communities.

“NextGEN: YouthPreneurs” was an important stage in implementing the Erasmus+ accreditation of Asociația Inițiative Sociale, contributing to its strategic objectives on entrepreneurial skills, social inclusion, intercultural cooperation and stronger international partnerships.

Through the involvement of 55 people from 8 countries, the 7 practice firms, the business plans and the promotional materials, the mobility showed the value of non-formal education in encouraging initiative, creativity and entrepreneurial thinking.

The fact that 88.7% of respondents want to start a business in the future, together with the positive evaluation and dissemination results, highlights the project’s contribution to young people’s entrepreneurial motivation and confidence in their own abilities.

The experience and skills developed through A3 are a foundation for turning business ideas into future initiatives and for young people’s active involvement in developing their communities.

The story continues! Together we are stronger!
