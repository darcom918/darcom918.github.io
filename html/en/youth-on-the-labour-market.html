From 6 to 16 June 2024, Asociația Inițiative Sociale (AIS) hosted the international youth exchange “InnoVenture: You(th) on the Labour Market” (A2) in Predeal, Romania. It was the second mobility carried out under the Erasmus+ accreditation in the field of youth (2022-1-RO01-KA150-YOU-000110034), through project 2023-1-RO01-KA151-YOU-000131090.

The mobility brought together 56 people from 9 countries (Bulgaria, Czechia, Greece, Italy, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Spain and Turkey): 45 young people aged 18 to 30, 9 group leaders and 2 facilitators. Inclusion was an important component: 19 young people with fewer opportunities took part (42.2% of all young participants), and the project gave them access to international opportunities for learning and professional development.

About the mobility

The main aim of the mobility was to develop employability skills and to improve young people’s ability to access job opportunities and become active on the labour market. The project set out to develop the skills needed in recruitment processes, build self-confidence, encourage personal initiative and strengthen intercultural cooperation.

Over 9 days of educational activities, the participants took part in an intensive non-formal education programme based on workshops, simulations, brainstorming, debates, role play, case studies, work in multicultural teams, reflection and participatory evaluation.

The activities included team-building and inclusion sessions, presentations on young people’s situation on the labour market in the nine countries, self-awareness and career guidance workshops, and practical job-preparation exercises.

Using tools such as SWOT analysis, the IKIGAI diagram and the RIASEC career test, the participants analysed their skills, interests, strengths and career aspirations, and identified development opportunities that fit their own profile.

Special emphasis was placed on workshops on writing a Europass CV and a cover letter, preparing for job interviews and building a personal brand.

One of the central activities was the “Job Fair”, a detailed simulation of a careers fair in which the young people played both employer and candidate. They prepared application documents, took part in mock interviews and received feedback on how they present and make the most of their skills.

The programme also included a study visit to the Bran–Brașov area, where participants explored local economic opportunities and met young employees, as well as intercultural activities, digital workshops and sessions on the Erasmus+ programme and the Youthpass tool.

A2 produced concrete educational results and materials, developed with the direct involvement of the participants:

  • 9 case studies and national presentations on youth unemployment, employability challenges and job opportunities in the participating countries;
  • 45 young people involved in hands-on work on Europass CVs, cover letters and the documents needed for recruitment;
  • 1 detailed Job Fair simulation, including applications for fictional jobs, interviews and candidate assessment;
  • 7 thematic videos made by multicultural teams about the challenges young people face when looking for work and in the workplace;
  • 1 project brochure and 1 blog dedicated to the mobility;
  • 9 national dissemination reports, with photos, videos, articles, presentations and testimonials.

Through these activities, the participants developed their skills in communication, public speaking, self-awareness, career planning, leadership, teamwork, critical thinking, adaptability and problem-solving.

They also gained practical knowledge of finding job opportunities, using the Europass tools, preparing applications and taking part in interviews.

The intercultural activities and the digital materials helped improve language, digital and social skills. The learning process was monitored through daily reflections and evaluations, and the skills gained were recognised through Youthpass.

The mobility was coordinated by AIS together with its 8 international partner organisations, through a structured system of planning, monitoring and evaluation.

Preparation included selecting the participants, signing the partnership agreements, designing the educational programme, arranging travel and accommodation at the Holiday Panoramic complex in Predeal, and preparing information and learning materials.

Management was supported by digital tools such as Google Drive, WhatsApp, Facebook and online collaboration platforms. Meetings with group leaders, daily reflections and the mid-term and final evaluations made it possible to monitor progress, spot difficulties and adapt the activities.

Special attention was paid to including young people with fewer opportunities, active participation, safety and environmentally responsible practices.

The post-mobility evaluation, based on 53 completed questionnaires, showed a high level of satisfaction and positive contributions to personal and professional development.

Satisfaction and skills assessment (scale from 1 to 4 points, where 4 is the highest score):

  • Overall satisfaction: 3.87 out of 4 points, a very high appreciation of the Erasmus+ experience.
  • Contribution to personal development: 3.70 out of 4 points, showing positive effects on self-confidence and personal growth.
  • Ability to work in multicultural teams: 3.74 out of 4 points.
  • Usefulness of the CV and cover letter workshops: 3.53 out of 4 points.
  • Usefulness of the job interview preparation sessions: 3.60 out of 4 points.
  • Contribution of the employability activities to professional skills: 3.57 out of 4 points.
  • Usefulness of the career guidance test: 3.55 out of 4 points.

Positive changes and skills gained (53 respondents):

  • 92.5% of respondents (49 of 53) noticed positive personal changes after taking part in the project.
  • 92.5% of respondents (49 of 53) confirmed that the educational materials helped develop their employability skills.
  • 100% of respondents (53 of 53) felt the project fostered a sense of community and collaboration among participants.
  • 100% of respondents (53 of 53) said they would like to take part in similar projects in the future.
  • 94.3% of respondents (50 of 53) clearly confirmed that they felt supported by the host organisation.

One of the most relevant results of A2 was that the skills gained during the mobility carried over into the participants’ careers.

In the post-mobility evaluation, 16 of the 53 respondents (30.2%) said they had found a job after taking part in the project.

This is a concrete indicator of the professional progress reported by participants. Although getting a job can depend on many factors, the result highlights the relevance of the employability skills covered during the mobility.

As for applying for jobs and attending real interviews after the mobility, the available data do not yet show the exact number of people. These results should be kept separate from the mock applications and interviews of the “Job Fair” learning activity.

By preparing CVs and cover letters, identifying job opportunities, taking part in mock interviews and developing self-branding skills, the project gave young people practical tools to approach real recruitment processes with more confidence.

Feedback from the group leaders confirmed that the activities were relevant to the participants’ professional development. According to the final Erasmus+ report, 9 out of 9 leaders said they were “Very satisfied” with the mobility.

All nine leaders also felt that the participants had clarified their career aspirations and that their job prospects had improved, and seven leaders felt the young people were better prepared to continue education or training.

The evaluation by the 8 partner organisations confirmed the quality of management and collaboration, with scores of 5 out of 5 for communication with partners, 4.88 out of 5 for collaboration between organisations and 5 out of 5 for meeting the project’s objectives.

The mobility also helped build AIS’s organisational capacity and grow its international network with 3 new partner organisations: New Society from Bulgaria, Mladoevropani from Czechia and Hawk Stars from Portugal.

The project results were promoted through the dedicated blog, social media, educational materials and information events organised by participants and partner organisations.

For A2, 7 dissemination and information sessions were reported, with 163 attendances in total, held in Romania, Czechia, Italy, Poland, Portugal and Spain. They included presentations on Erasmus+ opportunities, career guidance workshops and practical demonstrations of how to use Europass.

These were complemented by online promotion campaigns and materials made by participants from the nine countries on Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok and the partner organisations’ platforms.

The A2 blog had 1,521 visitors at the time of the final report in 2024, raising the project’s visibility and promoting Erasmus+ opportunities and non-formal education.

The materials were made publicly available, giving other young people and organisations access to the information and educational resources developed in the project.

“InnoVenture: You(th) on the Labour Market” was an important stage in implementing the Erasmus+ accreditation of Asociația Inițiative Sociale, helping to develop employability skills, promote social inclusion and intercultural cooperation, and strengthen international partnerships.

Through the involvement of 56 people from 9 countries, the practical skills developed by the 45 young people, the educational materials and the positive evaluation results, the mobility showed the value of non-formal education in preparing young people for the challenges of the labour market.

The 16 people who reported finding a job after the mobility are one of the most important post-project results, showing the professional progress made by some of the participants.

Together with the first mobility, “InnoVenture: You(th) in Business” (A1), dedicated to entrepreneurship, A2 completed the first year of the Erasmus+ accreditation with an integrated approach to developing skills for working life.

The experience gained laid the groundwork for the next AIS activities on entrepreneurship, employability, inclusion and the active participation of young people.

The story continues! Together we are stronger!
