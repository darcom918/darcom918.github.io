Description

In the third year, through LevelUP, AIS implemented activities dedicated to the community, career guidance and planning one's own path. Participants worked hands-on, collaborated in international teams and connected the Erasmus+ experience with local, community and professional realities.

Participants developed communication, teamwork, critical thinking, reflection, self-confidence, initiative, adaptability, intercultural cooperation and the ability to plan their educational and professional future more consciously.

From 23 October to 2 November 2025, Asociația Inițiative Sociale (AIS) hosted the international youth exchange “LevelUP: Ready4Work” (A6), the sixth mobility carried out under the Erasmus+ accreditation in the field of youth (2022-1-RO01-KA150-YOU-000110034), through project 2025-1-RO01-KA151-YOU-000299424.

According to the interim report, the mobility brought together 45 people from 7 countries (Czechia, Greece, Hungary, Italy, Lithuania, Poland and Romania): 36 young people, 7 group leaders and 2 facilitators.

Inclusion was a priority: 22 young people with fewer opportunities took part (61.1% of all young participants) and benefited from international opportunities for learning and professional development.

About the mobility

The main aim of the mobility was to develop employability skills and improve young people’s chances of entering the labour market, while promoting inclusion, diversity, intercultural cooperation and active participation.

Over 9 days of educational activities, the participants took part in an intensive non-formal education programme based on workshops, simulations, role play, debates, forum theatre, presentations, work in multicultural teams and participatory reflection.

The activities included presentations on the situation of young people on the labour market in the seven countries (Ready4Work in My Country), workshops on identifying skills and on writing and improving CVs (Your CV, Your Story), interview preparation (Interview Checklist) and an international recruitment simulation (Job Fair).

In the Job Fair simulation, 6 participants took on the role of recruiters, giving their peers the chance to experience interviews and to receive individual feedback on how they present themselves professionally and communicate with employers.

The programme also included study visits to the Aqua Carpatica factory and Vatra Dornei City Hall, where the young people discovered a real working environment, what employers expect and the career opportunities in the private sector and in public administration.

Intercultural activities, a tour of Vatra Dornei and sessions on Erasmus+ and Youthpass completed the learning experience.

A6 produced educational results and practical employability tools, created through the direct involvement of the participants:

  • 7 national presentations on the labour market and the challenges young people face.
  • Individual CVs written or improved, and tools for interview preparation.
  • 1 international Job Fair simulation, with recruiter and candidate roles.
  • 6 Ready4Oscars educational videos, against a minimum of 5 planned, exceeding the target by 20%.
  • 1 educational brochure, photo and video materials, and testimonials.
  • 7 national dissemination reports.

The six videos tackled relevant topics such as unemployment, competition for jobs, bullying and abuse at work, job-seeking strategies and work–life balance.

Through these activities, the young people developed their professional communication, creativity, critical thinking, adaptability, problem-solving, teamwork and digital skills.

The learning process was monitored through daily reflection and participatory evaluation, using the Youthpass approach to identify and recognise the skills gained.

The mobility was coordinated by AIS together with the 6 international partner organisations, through a structured system of preparation, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

The activities took place at Pensiunea Siady in Argestru, Vatra Dornei, with the support of the AIS team, the facilitators and the group leaders.

Management included educational and intercultural preparation, logistics, coordination meetings, initial, mid-term and final evaluations, and continuous monitoring of learning progress.

Special attention was paid to inclusion, equal participation, safety and adapting the activities to the young people’s needs.

The post-mobility evaluation, based on 42 questionnaires, showed a high level of satisfaction and positive contributions to personal and professional development.

Key evaluation results:

  • 4.81 out of 5 – overall satisfaction with the Erasmus+ experience.
  • 4.50 out of 5 – perceived contribution to personal development.
  • 83.3% (35 of 42) identified positive personal changes.
  • 97.6% (41 of 42) confirmed that the educational materials developed their employability skills.
  • 100% (42 of 42) felt that the interview preparation and the Job Fair simulation made them better prepared and more confident with employers.
  • 95.2% (40 of 42) confirmed that the workshops helped them improve their CVs.
  • 97.6% (41 of 42) valued the study visits for understanding a real working environment.
  • 100% (42 of 42) said they would like to take part in similar projects.

One of the most important results of A6 was how respondents put the skills they gained to use in their careers after the mobility.

The 42 post-mobility questionnaires showed the following outcomes:

  • 6 respondents found a full-time job;
  • 2 respondents found a part-time job;
  • 4 respondents were invited to at least one real job interview;
  • 1 respondent found an internship or volunteering opportunity;
  • 4 respondents were actively looking for a job.

In total, 8 respondents (19% of the 42) reported getting a job after the mobility, and others made progress in recruitment processes or in finding professional opportunities.

These are career outcomes reported after A6; not every job obtained is attributed solely to the project. The number of applications sent and actual attendance at the interviews respondents were invited to were not measured separately.

Feedback from the group leaders confirmed the relevance of the mobility: 6 of the 7 leaders answered “Very satisfied” and one “Rather satisfied”. All of them confirmed the benefits of taking part and the development of personal and professional skills.

The evaluation by the 6 partner organisations highlighted the quality of the collaboration and implementation, with 5/5 for cooperation with AIS and 4.83/5 for meeting the objectives and for the quality of the organisation and the educational programme. All partners said they would like to keep working together.

The results of A6 were promoted through public presentations, workshops, educational meetings and online campaigns run by the participants and partner organisations.

After the mobility, 8 dissemination sessions were held in the 7 participating countries, with 140 reported attendances.

They included two sessions in Romania (40 attendances) and presentations in Poland (30), Hungary (22), Italy – University of Trieste (20), Lithuania (11), Greece – Tutoring Center Alpha (9) and Czechia (8).

The dissemination activities took place in these locations:

  • Romania – 2 sessions, 40 attendances: the first took place in Craiova on 13 November 2025, run by Adriana and Minodora for 20 volunteers of the local organisation. The second was held on 8 November 2025 by Olivia, at a youth centre, with 20 students.
  • Poland – 1 session, 30 attendances: a presentation at a primary school in Łódź on 27–28 November 2025 for year 8 pupils, including the Erasmus+ experience and a creative workshop on the skills needed on the labour market.
  • Hungary – 1 session, 22 attendances: a presentation at the University of Pécs for social work and social policy students, with practical examples of improving CVs and a screening of the educational video Work Fight Club.
  • Italy – 1 session, 20 attendances: a presentation at the University of Trieste for students interested in international Erasmus+ opportunities, employability skills and non-formal education.
  • Lithuania – 1 session, 11 attendances: a presentation at the Art School of Panevėžys for students aged 17–18 on youth unemployment, preparing CVs, interview simulations and the benefits of international mobility.
  • Greece – 1 session, 9 attendances: a presentation at Tutoring Center Alpha for students preparing for the national university entrance exams, focusing on Erasmus+ opportunities, career guidance and labour-market challenges.
  • Czechia – 1 session, 8 attendances: a presentation at Lingua Centrum in Olomouc on 18 November 2025 for a group of students, with a presentation of the mobility and a discussion on youth unemployment, professional skills and Erasmus+ opportunities.

These events helped promote the educational results of A6, raised awareness of European mobility opportunities and encouraged other young people to take part in non-formal education.

“LevelUP: Ready4Work” contributed directly to the four strategic objectives of the AIS Erasmus+ accreditation: developing professional skills, promoting inclusion and diversity, strengthening organisational capacity and expanding international cooperation.

Through the involvement of 45 people from 7 countries, including 22 young people with fewer opportunities, the 6 educational videos and the positive evaluation results, the mobility showed the value of non-formal education in preparing young people for the labour market.

The 8 jobs reported after the mobility, together with the interview invitations, the professional development opportunities and the 140 attendances at dissemination events, show how A6 improved employability and multiplied its results in the partner communities.

The experience confirms how important it is to keep investing in non-formal education, inclusion and the skills young people need to build a sustainable professional future.

The story continues! Together we are stronger!
